SnowEx 8100PP: Truck Fit, Mounting and Parts for an 8-to-10-Foot Plow

The SnowEx 8100PP suits mixed commercial routes. Compare actual working widths, check the exact truck in Power Match, and price the Automatixx mount and storm-season parts.

Durand’s installation perspective

It’s a strong candidate for a mixed route with narrow approaches and open lots. It extends from 8 feet compact to 10 feet straight and weighs about 970 lb before the mount. Start with a suitable heavy-duty truck, then confirm the exact configuration in Power Match before buying the plow or a used mount.

For the Full Flow installation enquiry, start with the truck and what comes with the blade. On a used setup, photograph the tags, mount, plugs and controller. That lets Durand identify the parts that need fitment review before the truck takes up a shop bay. SnowEx supplies the model figures below.

Where the 8100PP earns its place

Choose it for a route where the same truck must negotiate a constrained entrance and then clear a larger paved lot. A shopping centre with narrow drive lanes and broad rows, or a strata with a tight entrance leading to visitor parking, is a good example. Compact mode gives the operator room to reposition; extended straight mode gives a wider pass when the area opens up. The forward-wing scoop position is useful when the job is to carry snow toward a designated pile.

A route made up mostly of tight driveways may favour a lighter straight blade. If the jobs are predominantly open lots, compare the heavier 8611PP, which SnowEx lists at 1,036 lb before the mount and up to 11 feet straight in wide-pass mode. Those figures are for the larger model. Review fit, turning room and snow storage for the route before choosing either blade. SnowEx model comparison.

Measure working width in the mode you’ll use

The 8100PP’s 10-foot figure applies in straight wide-pass mode. At full angle, that same mode clears 8 feet 10 inches. If your route depends on an angled pass beside a curb, plan from the angled figure. For gates or ramps, check the physical envelope, blade corners and truck turning room on site. A clearing-width figure alone cannot tell you whether the whole rig can manoeuvre through an opening. SnowEx working-width table.

Which trucks should you look at?

Think properly configured three-quarter-ton and larger trucks as a starting point. The exact 77750 row lists compatible Class 2–5 trucks and tractors. An F-250 or F-350, Ram 2500 or 3500, or Silverado/Sierra 2500HD or 3500HD is a candidate to check. The badge on the truck cannot establish fit. SnowEx lists about 970 lb for the plow, with the vehicle mount excluded. The installer needs to assess installed front-axle loading, the truck’s ratings and the rest of the winter setup.

Send the shop the VIN or the full year, make, model, cab, engine, drivetrain, front axle rating and snowplow-prep details. Mention any spreader or other equipment already fitted. Ask the installer to run the exact configuration in SnowEx Power Match and confirm the approved mounting hardware, completed weight and any manufacturer-specified ballast. A different cab, engine or build can change the answer.

What mounts to the truck and what stays with the blade?

Automatixx is SnowEx’s attachment system. It lets the operator attach and detach the plow from one side of the truck and uses removable receiver brackets. The vehicle mount is separate and has to match the exact truck. SnowEx’s platform also uses FLEET FLEX controls. Ask the installer to itemize the vehicle mount and brackets, truck-side wiring and lighting components, controller, and any configuration-specific equipment. When comparing bids, check that each shop is pricing the same truck-side package and installation scope. SnowEx attachment and control details.

A used plow with a controller and a pile of brackets is a starting inventory. Photograph the plow label and serial, the attachment assembly, connectors, lamps and controller. Have the shop confirm which parts suit your truck and whether the remaining hardware is available. Do not assume a mount or harness transfers from the seller’s truck or from another brand.

Can you get parts during a storm?

SnowEx publishes an illustrated parts catalogue and routes purchases through authorized dealers. The 8100PP / 77750 blade list includes the main steel cutting edge (B52067), standard steel wing edge (B51104), and model-specific wing and slide-box components. Use the serial and assembly numbers to confirm revisions and hardware before buying replacements. SnowEx 8100PP parts diagrams.

Ask the local dealer what is on the shelf through winter and what needs to be ordered: main and wing edges, wear strips, pins, hydraulic components, electrical connectors and control parts. Ask whether they provide in-season service and what lead times look like for the larger wing parts. A catalogue listing identifies a part. The dealer can confirm local inventory and service availability. SnowEx dealer locator · SnowEx parts and service.

What to send Durand before the installation quote

Bring the truck details, photos of any existing mount and electrical hardware, the blade and attachment serials if used, and measurements of the tightest parts of your route. Tell the installer where you angle, scoop or make wide straight passes and where you stack snow. Request an itemized fitment quote and ask what the shop checks at handover.
