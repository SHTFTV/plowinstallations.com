Will a Snow Plow Void Your Truck Warranty?

November 24, 2026

A snow plow doesn't usually cancel your whole truck warranty, but it can cost you coverage on the parts it affects if the truck wasn't built or approved for one. GM's owner information is blunt: if the vehicle doesn't have the snow plow prep package, adding a plow can damage it, and those repairs would not be covered by warranty. Ford recommends that Super Duty trucks used for snow removal include its Snow Plow Prep Package. In practice, the safest path is a truck ordered with the manufacturer's plow-ready option (or an approved dealer conversion), a plow within the published weight limits for your model, and an installation that follows the plow maker's and truck maker's instructions.

Here's what that means when you're shopping for a plow or a truck this winter.

What the manufacturers actually say

GM (Chevrolet Silverado, GMC Sierra)

GM's snow plow prep package carries the option code RPO VYU. A GM technical bulletin describes the light-duty package as including a higher-output generator, provisions for a roof-mounted lamp, a high-capacity air cleaner, a transfer case shield and heavy-duty front springs. The same bulletin says it's not recommended to add a plow to any vehicle without RPO VYU.

A Silverado 1500 owner's manual notice says that if the vehicle doesn't have the prep package, adding a plow can damage the vehicle and the repairs would not be covered by warranty. It adds that even with the package, payload is reduced once a plow is installed, and that exceeding the front or rear axle ratings or the gross vehicle weight rating can damage the truck.

For heavy-duty 2500 and 3500 trucks built without VYU, a GM bulletin described a dealer procedure to retrofit front suspension parts up to the package's requirements, with a matching revised front axle rating. That bulletin listed the conversion as a customer-pay job.

Ford (F-Series and Super Duty)

Ford's owner's manuals for Super Duty trucks say Ford recommends that trucks used for snow removal include the snow plow package, and they point owners to weight limits and installation guidelines. The manuals also note that rear ballast may be needed to avoid exceeding the front gross axle weight rating and to keep weight balanced for braking and steering.

Ford Canada's Super Duty upfit page lists its plow package with computer-selected springs for plow use. When Ford introduced its updated package for 2020 Super Duty, it described heavy-duty springs specific to each configuration and an uprated alternator, along with a maximum plow rating of 1,400 pounds for properly equipped gas models.

Light-duty trucks are more limited. When Ford offered a plow-ready package on the 2015 F-150, Cars.com reported a recommended maximum plow weight of 450 pounds for that truck.

Why prep packages matter

A plow hangs a lot of weight ahead of the front axle and draws heavy electrical loads. The packages address exactly those stresses:

Stress from plowing What prep packages typically add Risk without it
Extra weight on the front axle Heavier front springs, revised front axle rating Sagging, suspension and steering wear
High electrical demand when raising and angling Higher-output alternator or generator, wiring provisions Charging problems, electrical faults
Heat at low speed Cooling or air intake upgrades on some models Overheating during long pushes
Underbody impacts Skid or transfer case shielding on some models Damaged components

When a component fails and the truck wasn't equipped or approved for a plow, a dealer may decide the failure was plow-related. That's where coverage disputes start.

How to protect your warranty

  1. Check the build sheet. Confirm whether your truck has the manufacturer's plow package. The option code is on the window sticker or build information.
  2. Read your owner's manual and upfitter guide. GM and Ford both publish plow weight limits by model and configuration. Use the limit for your exact truck, not a general number.
  3. Weigh it, don't guess. Compare the front axle weight with the plow mounted against the front gross axle weight rating on the door sticker. Add rear ballast where the manual calls for it.
  4. Choose a plow approved for your truck. Plow makers publish vehicle application guides that match blades and mounts to specific models.
  5. Use a qualified installer. Wiring and mounting done to the plow maker's instructions reduces the chance of electrical problems that could be blamed on the install.
  6. Keep records. Save the installation invoice, plow model and weight, and any ballast details. If a claim is questioned, documentation helps.
  7. Ask before you buy. If you're ordering a new truck, ask the dealer in writing what plow use is supported on that exact configuration.

Buying a used truck to plow with

Many used trucks are sold with a plow already mounted. Ask whether the truck was built with the prep package, check the door sticker ratings, and look for signs of overloading such as uneven front ride height or worn front-end parts. A truck that's been plowing beyond its ratings may have shortened component life even if warranty is no longer a concern.

The bottom line

A plow on a properly equipped, correctly loaded truck shouldn't put your warranty at risk. A plow on a truck without the manufacturer's prep package, or one that pushes the front axle past its rating, can leave you paying for repairs a warranty would otherwise cover. Check the build, follow the published limits, and keep the paperwork.

Frequently asked questions

Does putting a snow plow on my truck void the warranty?

Usually not the entire warranty, but repairs to parts affected by the plow may be denied if the truck lacks the manufacturer's snow plow prep package or the plow exceeds published limits. GM's manual states repairs wouldn't be covered without its prep package.

What is a snow plow prep package?

It's a factory option that equips a truck for plowing, typically with heavier front springs and a higher-output alternator, plus other model-specific upgrades. GM calls it RPO VYU; Ford offers a Snow Plow Prep Package on Super Duty trucks.

Can I plow with a half-ton truck?

Only within the manufacturer's limits. GM recommends plowing only with light-duty trucks equipped with its prep package, and Ford's past F-150 package carried a low maximum plow weight. Check your owner's manual for your exact truck.

How do I check if my truck can handle a plow's weight?

Compare the front axle weight with the plow mounted against the front gross axle weight rating on the driver's door sticker, and follow the manual's guidance on rear ballast.
