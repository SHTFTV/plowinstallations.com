Plow Mount Types: What Changes Between Brands

November 27, 2026 · PlowInstallations Editorial

Every truck plow has two halves that meet at the mount: a vehicle-specific bracket or undercarriage bolted to the truck frame, and the plow's own hitch (often called headgear or an A-frame) that latches onto it. What changes between brands is the shape of that connection, how it latches, how much hardware stays on the truck in summer and which wiring goes with it. Fisher's Minute Mount 2 and Western's UltraMount 2 use a drive-in, mechanical latch; BOSS uses its SmartHitch 2 system with a BOSS undercarriage. Within a brand family, plows often share a mount design. Across brands, the truck-side hardware generally isn't interchangeable, so the mount you install now largely decides which plows you can hang on that truck later.

The two halves of every mount

Truck side. This is the part your installer bolts to the frame rails behind the bumper. Manufacturers engineer it for a specific make, model and year range. Fisher says each of its vehicle mount kits is engineered for an exact fit and certified to meet applicable Federal and Canadian Motor Vehicle Safety Standards. BOSS describes its undercarriages the same way: they mount to the vehicle frame and are the main attachment point for the plow.

Plow side. The blade, lift frame and headgear. This half is generally designed to fit any truck that has the matching mount for that system, which is why a plow can move from an old truck to a new one if the new truck gets the right vehicle kit.

Wiring. Lighting and control harnesses cross the same line. Some parts are universal within a system and some are tied to the truck's headlamps.

How the major systems compare

System Brand How it connects What stays on the truck
Minute Mount 2 Fisher Drive forward until the mount engages the headgear, then lock spring-loaded pins Vehicle-specific mount; receiver brackets
UltraMount 2 Western Same drive-in mechanical approach Vehicle-specific mount; receiver brackets can be removed without tools
SmartHitch 2 BOSS Hitch engages a BOSS undercarriage BOSS undercarriage and harness

Fisher and Western are sister brands under Douglas Dynamics, which is why their current mounting systems work so similarly. Western describes UltraMount 2 as built on an A-frame/T-frame with three-inch square tubing, and says its receiver brackets come off without tools to maximize ground clearance in the off-season. Fisher's hookup instructions for MM2 boil down to driving into the headgear, swinging the disconnect up, locking the pins, raising the jack and plugging in.

On the BOSS side, the company has long promoted a universal undercarriage and wiring harness that works with BOSS plows using SmartHitch 2 or RT3, so an operator running both a V-plow and a straight blade from BOSS can swap blades on the same truck.

What changes when you switch brands

  • The truck-side mount. A Fisher mount won't accept a BOSS plow, and vice versa. Switching brands usually means a new vehicle kit.
  • Electrical connectors. Each brand has its own plugs and modules, so the grille plugs and under-hood components normally change too.
  • Controller. In-cab controls are brand-specific.
  • Clearance and appearance. How much bracket hangs below the bumper in summer differs by system, and it matters if you drive the truck off-road or tow.

What changes even within one brand

Staying with a brand doesn't always mean everything carries over. Fisher's guide to plow transfers notes that:

  • The parts that vary from truck to truck are the mount, the headlamp harness and the isolation module.
  • The original Minute Mount and MM2 differ: the original has a pin on each side that must be pulled separately, while the newer version releases both pins with one handle.
  • 2007 and newer Chevy, GMC and Toyota Tundra trucks may not be compatible with the original Minute Mount because of headgear and bumper fittings.
  • If your truck doesn't appear in the brand's fit tool (Fisher calls theirs eMatch), there is no approved plow application for it under the manufacturer's guidelines.

That last point matters more than any feature comparison. Brands set approved applications by vehicle, partly because of front axle weight limits, so a heavier plow that fits physically may still not be approved for your truck.

Questions to ask before you choose a mount

  1. Is my exact truck listed? Check the brand's fit tool with your year, cab, engine and package.
  2. Will this mount still be supported in five years? Older mount generations can get harder to source.
  3. Do I plan to run more than one blade? Staying within one system lets you swap plows on the same truck.
  4. How will the truck be used in summer? If ground clearance or appearance matters, ask how much hardware remains and whether brackets come off.
  5. What happens when I replace the truck? Ask what it would take to move the plow to the next vehicle: usually a new mount kit and possibly a new headlamp harness and module.
  6. Who services it locally? Dealer support and parts stock in the Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley count for a lot in a January storm.

Book early

Mount kits are vehicle-specific, and the right one is not always on a dealer's shelf. Fisher's own advice is to contact a dealer in summer or early fall for a new installation or transfer, because once winter arrives dealers are busy and parts can be hard to find. If you're adding a plow this season, get your truck's details to an installer now so the correct kit can be confirmed and ordered.

The bottom line

Pick the plow brand and the mount together, because the truck-side hardware commits you to a system. Confirm your vehicle is an approved application, understand which wiring parts are tied to your headlamps, and ask what a future transfer would involve. A mount chosen carefully now saves a lot of rework when you change trucks or add a second blade.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a BOSS plow on a Fisher mount?

Generally no. Truck-side mounts are specific to each brand's hitch system, so switching brands normally means installing a new vehicle mount kit and brand-specific wiring.

Are Fisher and Western mounts the same?

Fisher and Western are sister brands under Douglas Dynamics, and their current Minute Mount 2 and UltraMount 2 systems work in a very similar drive-in, mechanical way. Confirm compatibility for your specific plow and truck with a dealer.

What happens if my truck isn't in the plow maker's fit tool?

According to Fisher, if a vehicle isn't listed in its eMatch tool, there is no approved plow application for it under the company's guidelines.

Can I move my plow to a new truck?

Often yes, but the new truck usually needs its own vehicle-specific mount, and the headlamp harness and isolation module may also need to change.
