Plow Mount Types: What Changes Between Brands

November 27, 2026 · PlowInstallations Editorial

A front-mounted truck-plow system pairs a vehicle-specific mount or undercarriage with the plow-side coupling and lift components. The connection, latch, remaining truck hardware and electrical package differ by brand and generation. FISHER Minute Mount 2, WESTERN UltraMount 2 and BOSS SmartHitch 2 name different attachment systems; none is a universal approval for a truck and blade combination. Confirm the exact vehicle, plow model, mount and electrical application together before buying or transferring equipment.

The two halves of every mount

Truck side. The mount or undercarriage connects the system to the vehicle. FISHER describes its vehicle mount kits as engineered for an exact fit. Identify the required kit through the manufacturer's vehicle application information, including the actual model year and configuration. A bracket that looks right is not proof of an approved installation.

Plow side. Identify the blade, attachment generation and model or serial number. Headgear, coupler and A-frame refer to components with different functions; they are not interchangeable names for a universal hitch. A physically matching connection does not establish that the truck is approved for the blade, its installed load or its electrical system.

Wiring. Confirm the lighting, controls, harnesses and modules by part number and application. FISHER's plow-transfer guidance explains that electrical generations and vehicle headlamp types can require different components. Do not infer compatibility from the brand name or the shape of a connector.

How the major systems compare

System Brand System distinction What stays on the truck
Minute Mount 2 Fisher Mechanical attachment system with connecting pins Vehicle-specific mount; receiver brackets
UltraMount 2 Western Mechanical attachment system with a rotating pivot bar Vehicle-specific mount; receiver brackets can be removed without tools
SmartHitch 2 BOSS Hydraulic attachment system with couplers Application-specific BOSS undercarriage and electrical equipment

WESTERN's UltraMount 2 description identifies a mechanical attachment system, a rotating pivot bar and removable receiver brackets. WESTERN states compatibility with its UltraMount mounts and electrical platforms. That statement concerns the named WESTERN systems; it does not establish compatibility with FISHER Minute Mount 2 or approve every truck and blade combination. Similar attachment methods are not a parts interchange specification.

For BOSS, use the current Product Selector and model-specific manuals. BOSS identifies SmartHitch 2 as a hydraulic attachment system in its parts glossary. Confirm the exact undercarriage, controller and wiring for each proposed blade. A historical brochure's universal-system wording should not be used as current approval to swap any BOSS plows on a truck.

What changes when you switch brands

  • The truck-side mount. Treat a brand change as a new compatibility assessment. Ask for the manufacturer-approved vehicle kit and attachment combination; do not assume an existing mount accepts another brand.
  • Electrical connectors. Check the complete electrical package, including lighting and control modules. A plug connection alone does not establish compatibility.
  • Controller. Verify that the named controller and software or electrical generation, where applicable, support the exact plow.
  • Clearance and appearance. How much bracket hangs below the bumper in summer differs by system, and it matters if you drive the truck off-road or tow.

What changes even within one brand

Staying with a brand does not mean everything carries over. FISHER's transfer guidance highlights these checks:

  • The parts that vary from truck to truck are the mount, the headlamp harness and the isolation module.
  • The original Minute Mount and Minute Mount 2 are different attachment generations.
  • FISHER says a vehicle absent from its eMatch tool does not have an approved snowplow application under its guidelines. For another brand, follow that manufacturer's own application process and resolve an unclear result with its technical support.

That last point matters more than any feature comparison. Brands set approved applications by vehicle, partly because of front axle weight limits, so a heavier plow that fits physically may still not be approved for your truck.

Questions to ask before you choose a mount

  1. Is my exact truck listed? Check the brand's fit tool with your year, cab, engine and package.
  2. Will this mount still be supported in five years? Older mount generations can get harder to source.
  3. Do I plan to run more than one blade? Have each blade checked for the truck, mount and electrical package. A shared system name does not replace those checks.
  4. How will the truck be used in summer? If ground clearance or appearance matters, ask how much hardware remains and whether brackets come off.
  5. What happens when I replace the truck? Ask what it would take to move the plow to the next vehicle: usually a new mount kit and possibly a new headlamp harness and module.
  6. Who services it locally? Dealer support and parts stock in the Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley count for a lot in a January storm.

Book early

Give the installer the vehicle details, plow identification and any existing component numbers before booking. Ask for a parts list, what can be reused, what needs inspection and which items must be replaced. Confirm stock, lead time and appointment availability directly; a catalogue listing does not establish local availability.

Confirm the complete installation

Confirm the vehicle, blade, attachment generation and electrical package as one approved installation. Use the exact manufacturer instructions and a qualified installer for mounting, wiring, checks and handover; this comparison is not an installation procedure. Keep the part numbers and application records for future service or a truck transfer.

For a Lower Mainland installation enquiry, see Durand at Full Flow Hydraulics in Aldergrove and confirm specific coverage directly. For other locations, use the installer network enquiry. This general guide does not claim hands-on testing or manufacturer authorization by an individual installer.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a BOSS plow on a Fisher mount?

Do not assume compatibility. Have the exact vehicle, blade, mount and electrical application checked against the relevant manufacturer documents. A brand change requires a fresh compatibility assessment.

Are Fisher and Western mounts the same?

Do not treat Minute Mount 2 and UltraMount 2 as interchangeable. Similar attachment methods or shared corporate ownership do not establish parts compatibility. Confirm the exact system and vehicle application with the manufacturer or qualified installer.

What happens if my truck isn't in the plow maker's fit tool?

FISHER says an unlisted vehicle has no approved application under its eMatch guidelines. Follow each manufacturer's own application process and ask its technical support to resolve an unclear result before proceeding.

Can I move my plow to a new truck?

Only after the receiving truck and the complete equipment combination are confirmed as suitable. The mount, headlamp harness, modules or other components may need replacement, and used parts need condition checks.
