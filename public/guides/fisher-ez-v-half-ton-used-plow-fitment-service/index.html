FISHER EZ-V on a half-ton: fit, used buying and local service

Compare current EZ-V specifications, identify a used setup and prepare a vehicle-specific fitment and service enquiry for Durand at Full Flow Hydraulics.

PlowInstallations Editorial · October 29, 2026 · Manufacturer sources checked October 29

Does FISHER make a half-ton V-plow?

Yes. FISHER currently lists the 7-foot-6-inch EZ-V for half-ton trucks and tractors, subject to its vehicle-specific eMatch process. The current specification table identifies powder-coated model 95150 at approximately 510 lb and stainless model 95160 at approximately 550 lb. Those figures exclude the vehicle mount, which FISHER describes as approximately 60 lb for V-plows. They are not the complete installed load or approval for every half-ton pickup. Check the current manufacturer specification and eMatch link before discussing a particular truck.

Start with the truck, not the badge on the grille

A useful enquiry names the exact year, make, model, cab, engine and vehicle configuration, together with the vehicle’s stated weight ratings and any modifications. Ask the installer to check the complete proposed setup against the applicable manufacturer requirements, including front gross axle weight rating and gross vehicle weight rating. A successful installation on someone else’s Sierra or F-150 does not settle your own application. Save the actual matching result and discuss anything the tool does not resolve with the appropriate supplier.

What the width numbers mean for the conversation

FISHER lists a 7-foot-6-inch blade, but the current working widths vary with its position: 6 feet 7 inches at full angle, 6 feet 5 inches in scoop and 6 feet 9 inches in full V. These are manufacturer specifications, not measurements taken by us. Tell the shop about the entrances, parked vehicles and turning room on your route. The useful question is how a correctly approved setup serves that work, rather than whether a V-plow is always better than a straight blade.

Owner chatter: a useful question, not a fitment certificate

In a public r/Snowplow discussion, commenter Mikey-1884 describes using a FISHER EZ-V on a 2021 Sierra with the 3.0 diesel for their own driveway and neighbours, rather than commercial plowing. A second thread from the same username describes the setup as capable but narrower than a larger plow. These are self-reported anecdotes, not independently tested results or two separate owners establishing consensus. We have not substituted a commenter’s rough weight estimate for FISHER’s specification. Read the original half-ton discussion and the later truck discussion in context.

Buying used: do you need a special installer?

The practical priority is a shop experienced with the particular FISHER generation and truck configuration, with access to the correct documentation and parts. We have not established a universal legal requirement to use one particular class of shop. FISHER’s own transfer article directs owners toward its dealer network and explains that mounting generations, vehicle lighting and electrical systems can change what is transferable. A familiar-looking mount or a seller’s assurance that it fits a half-ton is not enough. Read FISHER’s transfer guidance and use its official support routes when factory-specific help is needed.

Ask what is included before agreeing a used price

Prepare a list of the plow model and serial identification, mounting generation, truck-side hardware, wiring, lights, controller and any loose parts included in the sale. Ask a qualified shop to identify the parts rather than guessing from a distant marketplace photograph. Request a condition assessment and an itemised installed-cost conversation: what can be reused, what must be replaced, what is missing and what remains unconfirmed. A low blade-only asking price and a complete installed quotation describe different purchases. This guide provides enquiry preparation, not instructions for wiring, mounting or operating the equipment.

Why the Northeast connection comes up

Douglas Dynamics identifies FISHER as founded in 1948 in Rockland, Maine, and describes its New England heritage. In Arundel, Maine, Weirs Motor Sales describes itself as one of New England’s largest FISHER distributors; that is the dealer’s own claim. FISHER’s directory separately lists Weirs as a Factory Direct Dealer with Master Mechanic recognition. The dealer’s size description is self-reported; Northeast visibility does not establish British Columbia stock or service availability.

Maintenance starts with identifying what you own

Keep the correct model and serial information with service records, relevant symptoms and the manual for the actual equipment generation. Tell the shop about previous repairs and changes you know about; mark unknown history as unknown. Ask what inspection and maintenance scope is appropriate for this setup instead of following a generic fluid, wiring or adjustment procedure online. Pair the discussion with the existing preseason enquiry checklist and the installation quote breakdown. No repair or fitment assessment has been performed through reading this page.
